Kartaan Click — settings

Portal check-ins, and accepting orders. Everything here is off until you switch it on.

Read this before switching it on

The platforms count how often you open your orders because they are trying to measure you. If this does the looking for you, the number stops being about you — and a platform that looked closely could take that view. It is your shop and your call; this is here so you make it knowingly.

Being straight about one more thing: the gap between rounds is random and so is the pause between clicks, and that is deliberate — a burst of instant clicks at the same minute every hour does not look like a person at the screen. Nothing is faked and nothing is hidden, but it is written to resemble you, and you should know that before switching it on.

It also only works while your browser is open. If a portal wants you to sign in, it presses that portal's own Log in button once — never if there is anywhere on the page to type a password, a phone number or a code, and it never types anything itself. If that does not get in, it leaves the tab open asking you to, and picks that portal up the moment you do.

Portal check-ins

Every so often, open each portal behind whatever you are doing, click through the order tabs the way you would, and leave the tab open — one per portal, already signed in, ready for next time.

A different, random gap each time, somewhere between those two — never the same beat over and over. Ten minutes is the closest together rounds will go; anything less is a lot of visits a day for a platform to look at.

Your working hours. No round starts outside them — the one exception is "Do one round now" below, which runs when you press it whatever the time.

Your orders page on each portal

You should not need to touch these. Flipkart and Amazon have built-in addresses, and Meesho's — which contains a code belonging to your own account — is picked up on its own the first time you are on your Meesho panel. Fill a box in only if a round is landing on the wrong page. Anything that is not a secure address on that portal's own site is ignored.

Accepting orders on its own — read this one twice

Everything above only looks at your orders. This part acts: it presses Accept. Accepting an order is a promise to dispatch it by a date, and that promise is yours, not this extension's. A penalty for missing it lands on your account.

So it is built to do nothing unless you have said yes three separate times: switch it on here, switch on the portal, and tick at least one SKU on that portal's own orders page. Miss any one of them and it accepts nothing at all and says so in the list at the bottom of this page.

If it cannot read when an order is due, it leaves that order alone. Always. A date it does not understand is never treated as "probably fine".

Accepting orders on its own

At the end of each round, work through the orders waiting to be accepted and accept the ones that pass your rules below. Flipkart and Meesho only.

This rides on the rounds above. Accepting happens at the end of a check-in round, so if check-ins are switched off, no round happens and nothing is ever accepted.

On which portals

Amazon is not here. Its orders move on by themselves once payment clears, so there is nothing to press — and that is being looked at properly before anything is promised.

Which SKUs

You tick them on the portal's own orders page, in the Kartaan Click panel: press Scan SKUs, tick the ones you are happy to take on, put a number beside any you want a daily limit on, then press Save ticks. With this box ticked and nothing ticked there, nothing is accepted — which is the safe way round. Untick this box and it will accept any SKU.

How soon they must be due

0 means only what is due today. 1 means today and tomorrow. An order due further out than this is left for later — there is no reason to take on next week's deadline today.

These are usually the urgent ones. Leave this on unless you would rather look at late orders yourself.

A backstop

The daily one is the important one. A round happens every 20 to 60 minutes, so "no more than 20 in one go" on its own would still be several hundred a day. The daily number counts every order accepted on that portal since this morning, including SKUs you left without a limit of their own, and it starts again each day.
